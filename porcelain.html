02. Editorial
Moodboard & Poster
The project began with an exploration of Qing dynasty porcelain, focusing on three things: the shapes of the vessels, their vivid glaze colors, and the history behind them. In Qing porcelain, color works as a visual language that carries symbolism, hierarchy, and aesthetic values. These studies became my first poster, built on contrast, form, and a color hierarchy drawn from cobalt blue, imperial yellow, famille rose, and celadon green.
A Little Book of Porcelain
A Little Book of Porcelain is a children’s book that introduces what porcelain is, how it is made, and its different types and colors, from blue-and-white to famille rose. The last pages become interactive exercises, such as a maze, tracing vessel shapes, matching colors, and designing your own vase, so children learn the history and characteristics of porcelain by doing.

Exhibition Campaign
Building on my early research, I developed a campaign for a virtual porcelain exhibition, including a motion piece and a series of social media posts. Because the exhibition is made for children, every element is kept simple and easy to read: clear vessel silhouettes, flat bright color, and friendly, playful typography.
Color Swatches
The swatch book studies five porcelain colors: Cobalt Underglaze Blue, Imperial Enamel Yellow, Famille Rose Blush, Celadon Mist Green, and Porcelain Body White, each printed on a paper chosen to echo its glaze. Unlike a typical swatch book, the cards sit on a laser-cut wooden stand carved with traditional porcelain patterns, displayed the way precious porcelain is shown in a museum. Vessel shapes are cut out of each card, so layering them reveals overlapping silhouettes.
